# §1.4 window sensitivity sweep

Generated by `python/window_sweep.py`. §1.4 declares five upper endpoints
(DOY 365, 385, 395, 410, 425) and asks for Gate 1-3 pass rates, the
extraction-failure rate and the §3.8 headline coefficient at each. The 425
arm is the run with no truncation at all, so it is simultaneously the
headline and the sweep's control.

## Control check: does no truncation reproduce the committed panel?

**Verdict: EPSILON.**

Every integer-valued and text column -- all four breakpoints, the
retention flag, the failure reason and the failure class -- is
bit-identical. The differences are confined to the continuous dB level
estimates, which come out of a weighted least-squares solve:

These are last-bit floating-point differences (relative ~1e-16, i.e.
the 16th significant figure of a double). Two candidate causes were
tested and **both refuted** on this machine: `--jobs` 1, 2 and 4 give a
byte-identical panel, and so does pinning the BLAS thread count to 1.
The remaining candidate is a difference in the numerical environment
between the container that produced the committed panel and the one that
produced the control -- OpenBLAS selects its reduction order from the
core count it sees. That is recorded as the untested candidate it is,
not as a finding.
